Redesigning the system behind 250M+ SKUs

When item data breaks, shelves go empty and legal exposure compounds. I led design for the platform that 600+ vendor-merchants use to onboard products across Albertsons' entire catalog, reducing setup time by 60% and mitigating $2.5B in compliance risk.

Collaborators 5 PMs · 20+ Engineers
Timeline 12 months · Shipped
My role

I owned the end-to-end redesign of the item creation and attribute management workflows. I led discovery with divisional merchants, national merchants, and MDS teams to map the complex multi-layer approval ecosystem.

My key contribution was partnering with the data science team to implement real-time validation across 10 of 11 critical attributes that directly addressed the 28.4% data mismatch rate. I also grew the component library by 60% using the Universal Design System (UDS), creating reusable patterns that partner and cost pods adopted for development feasibility.

Why This Was Hard

This looked like a form redesign. It wasn't.

Underneath a vendor onboarding problem were three colliding tensions that no single UX solution could fully resolve. Understanding that tension was the first design decision.

⚖️

Speed vs. compliance

Vendors needed to onboard items in hours. Legal required 11 validation checkpoints. Every shortcut created liability. Every checkpoint created abandonment.

👥

External vs. internal users

600+ external vendor-merchants and internal merchant teams used the same platform but needed completely different permission states, views, and mental models.

📊

160+ attributes vs. cognitive load

Every item required up to 160 data attributes. No chunking strategy existed. Vendors were submitting incomplete data not from negligence, but from overwhelm.

Signal

The legacy Salsify tool had a 28.4% mismatch rate on submissions. That's not a content problem. It's an information architecture problem pretending to be a compliance problem.

Research Reframed

What research actually changed

Field visits to distribution centers and interviews with 14 vendors and 9 merchant teams surfaced three findings that reframed the entire design direction.

We assumed

Vendors abandoned the form because it was too long

→
Research showed

Vendors abandoned because they couldn't tell how far they were or what remained. Length wasn't the issue. Progress legibility was.

We assumed

Merchants needed a purpose-built review dashboard different from what vendors saw

→
Research showed

Merchant-vendor back-and-forth grew from them looking at different representations of the same data. Mirroring the view eliminated communication overhead.

We assumed

Real-time validation would increase cognitive load during item entry

→
Research showed

Post-submission error correction was the real cost: vendors lost an average of 4.5 hours per rejected submission. Inline validation was the cheaper failure mode.

Design Decisions
01
Decision · Task accountability

Redesigned existing components for informed interactions

Considered
Autofilled forms to reduce manual workflow by vendors
Chose
Form restructuring and reusable components based on product timeline prioritization
Why
Decluttered the form designs for easy readability and helped vendors and merchants more accountable to changes and approvals in item onboarding stages by scaling existing component structures.
Tradeoff
Although the item onboarding process forms were lengthy in nature, the number of clicks were reduced with the new design and engineering team handoffs were faster.
Signal it worked
Vendor and merchant feedback revealed 60% time efficiency in attribute collection and approvals with less back and forth communication.
What I'd do differently

I'd validate phase labels with a card sort before engineering commit. We named them after data architecture, not vendor mental models. Renaming in sprint 3 cost a week.

02
Decision · Data validation

Informed validation vs. non actionable instances

Considered
Individual data validation modal for all compliance attributes
Chose
Inline validation rules and guided instructions for vendors
Why
I partnered with product and data science team to co-create validation rules for each non-negotiable attribute to inform vendors about certain show stopper attributes.
Tradeoff
Restarting the item setup process from a discontinued compliance form was unavoidable due to compliance industry standards.
Signal it worked
Fewer than 12% vendor and merchant interactions for compliance related communication. Error validation rates reduced by 78%.
What I'd do differently

We prioritized 10 of 11 attributes for v1. I'd have pushed for all 11. The remaining attribute had a 6% error rate that wasn't caught until post-launch.

Before

This form broke the user experience and created a gap in the item creation flow leading to back and forth vendor-merchant communication

After

The new form is made with clear inline guidance, content redesign and error states for next steps to vendors followed by email notifications

03
Decision · Asset management

Table format vs. card gallery for digital assets

Considered
Visual card/gallery layout for updating product images and digital assets
Chose
Table format validated via A/B testing, easily scalable for hierarchy structures
Why
The table format was solving 2 problems in one by addressing merchant image attribute requirements and marketing team needs that cut down on additional repository.
Tradeoff
Merging two product requirements into one. I negotiated with engineering and product teams to build a single repository that could enhance the product experience much better.
Signal it worked
Merchants and vendors cut down additional workaround for image capture by 85% and merchant portal remained single source of truth for item image based attributes.
What I'd do differently

The mirrored approach worked well, but we underestimated role-aware state complexity. I'd scope the MDS override flow as a separate track next time rather than folding it in.

If I did this again

Three things I'd do differently

01

Run a card sort before committing to IA

We named the onboarding phases after our data model (Draft, Pending, Approved), not how vendors think. A card sort before sprint 2 would have caught this. Renaming mid-engineering cost a full sprint and required re-documentation across 3 teams.

02

Push back harder on attribute de-scoping

De-scoping the 11th attribute felt like a reasonable sprint tradeoff. Post-launch data showed a 6% error rate from that specific gap. Incomplete coverage in a compliance system compounds. The timeline risk of including it would have been smaller than the fix.

03

Treat the merchant interface as a parallel workstream

We folded the MDS override flow into the vendor design track because the surfaces looked similar. The role-aware state complexity between vendors and merchants was significantly higher than anticipated. A dedicated merchant UX track from the start would have produced a cleaner, less patched outcome.
